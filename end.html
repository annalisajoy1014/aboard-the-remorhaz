— Day Nine —
Land­fall

The Remorhaz comes through the last ice channel just before noon. Nobody says anything at first. After nine days of grey water and grey sky and the creak of a hull you've learned to read like a voice, the sight of something that isn't ocean stops every sound on the ship.

Syrinlya. The last outpost before the interior of Foren. A handful of lights. Smoke from fires you can see from a mile out. A dock that has been fighting the ice for decades and is still, just barely, winning.

— The Remorhaz Crew —
A Ship That Carried You

Thorgrim brings the ship in with the quiet authority of someone who has done this a hundred times. Kalt is at the lines before the gangplank drops, jaw set, eyes checking every rope as it goes. Haldor leans on the rail and whistles at the size of the ice shelf to the north — appreciative, not afraid.

Ursula says nothing. She stands at the crow's nest long after she needs to, watching the dock come closer with an expression you can't quite read from below. Something between relief and something else entirely.

You've earned your place on this ship. Every one of them knows it now.

— Syrinlya —
The Edge of Everything

The outpost is smaller than you expected. Twenty souls, maybe less. Furs, rope, fire, and a silence that sits differently than the silence at sea — heavier, like the land itself is listening.

They come to the dock to watch the Remorhaz come in. Their faces are the faces of people who have been waiting for news, though none of them say what kind. Beyond them, past the settlement and the strange stone arch that stands alone at the edge of the ice like a door to somewhere the world forgot to name — the mountains of Foren rise white and immense and very, very old.

— Something Is Wrong —
The Woman at the Dock

One of them pulls you aside before you've cleared the gangplank. A woman with frost in her hair and a scar that crosses her jaw — someone who looks like she has survived things she hasn't told anyone about. She speaks quietly. The kind of quiet that means she doesn't want the others to hear.

— SHE SAYS —

"The last expedition into Foren left thirteen days ago. Twenty people. They should have returned in five."

— What She Pressed Into Your Hand —
The Compass

She presses something cold into your hand. A compass — old, brass-cased, good make. The needle spins freely, pointing nowhere, as if north has simply stopped mattering here.

Then she looks past you toward the interior of the island, toward the ruins that wait beneath two thousand years of ice, and her voice drops to almost nothing.

— SHE SAYS —

"Something has woken up under the ice. Something in the old city. We've heard it twice now, at night, when the wind drops. It doesn't sound like the wind. It doesn't sound like anything that should be alive."

"It sounds like it's looking for something."

— Foren Awaits —
The Voyage Is Complete.
The Adventure Has Not Yet Begun.

The Remorhaz creaks behind you at the dock, settling into the ice-cold water. The fires of Syrinlya burn low in the grey afternoon light. The broken stone arch stands silent at the edge of the settlement.

The mountains of Foren wait beyond it — vast, ancient, and keeping their own counsel. Whatever is buried beneath them has been patient for a very long time.

The compass in your hand does not stop spinning.

A greater adventure awaits on the island of Foren — if you dare to follow the compass into the dark.
